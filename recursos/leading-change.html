Mobilization Phase

Leading Change

Turn resistance to change (yours and your team’s) into an advantage, not a brake.

Level 1 · Open

Use cases

Communicate a restructuring without losing your team’s trust; lead a transformation when you yourself have doubts; hold the course when pressure demands immediate results.

Resistance isn't the enemy

Resistance to change usually means one of two things: something hasn't been explained clearly enough, or something hasn't been genuinely heard yet. Treating it as an obstacle to overcome, instead of information to use, is what turns manageable friction into open opposition. The leaders who move change fastest aren't the ones who push hardest — they're the ones who can tell which phase their people are actually in.

Try it this week

The 3-Phase Check

  1. Name which phase your change effort is actually in: Awareness (do people understand what's changing and why), Ownership (do they see what's in it for their day-to-day), or Execution (do they have the skills and resources to act on it).
  2. Ask three people directly affected which phase they think you're in — their answer, not yours, is the real diagnosis.
  3. If most of what you're hearing is about "why," don't push execution tools yet. You're skipping a phase, and it will resurface later as resistance.
