Mobilization Phase

Leading Change

Turn resistance to change (yours and your team’s) into an advantage, not a brake.

Level 1 · Open

Use cases

Communicate a restructuring without losing your team’s trust; lead a transformation when you yourself have doubts; hold the course when pressure demands immediate results.

Resistance isn't the enemy

According to McKinsey research, roughly 70% of organizational transformations fail — and of those, most fail not because the strategy was wrong, but because of unsupportive management behavior or unmanaged employee resistance. John Kotter's 8-step model exists because most change efforts skip steps under time pressure: leaders jump straight to communicating the vision without first building enough urgency or a coalition strong enough to sustain it. But the deeper reason resistance shows up isn't tactical, it's emotional. William Bridges' Transition Model makes the distinction that matters: change is the external event, but transition is the internal psychological process people have to go through — letting go of the old way, surviving a confusing in-between, and only then genuinely starting the new one. Skip the middle phase and the "resistance" you're seeing is often just people who haven't finished letting go yet.

Try it this week

The Transition Check

  1. Name which phase your change effort is actually in: Ending (are people still grieving what's being lost), Neutral Zone (are they confused about what to do now, with old ways gone and new ones not yet solid), or New Beginning (do they have the skills, clarity, and energy to fully commit).
  2. Ask three people directly affected which phase they think they're in — their answer, not yours, is the real diagnosis.
  3. If most of what you're hearing sounds like grief or disorientation, don't push execution tools yet. You're skipping the Neutral Zone, and it will resurface later as resistance.
