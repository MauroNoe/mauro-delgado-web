The moment

Your results got you here. The next level is decided differently.

In multinational companies, the step into senior leadership often runs through an assessment center: competency-based interviews, role plays, business cases and psychometric tests designed to observe how you think, decide and relate to others under pressure. I’ve worked with executives who now hold the positions they prepared for, and they almost always arrive in one of these situations:

The opportunity came sooner than expected

The role opens in a few weeks, the process has several stages and one of them is eliminatory. You need a plan, not just good intentions.

Role plays and uncomfortable questions worry you

You know what to say about your results. What creates tension is improvising in a difficult situation while someone is watching.

You know you’re good, but not whether it will show

Day to day, your work speaks for you. In an assessment you have a few hours to make it visible, without stopping being yourself.

My approach

Authenticity, not performance

Assessment center assessors are trained to look for consistent behaviour. A perfect but rehearsed answer shows, and it doesn’t hold up once you’re in the role. So the work isn’t about learning what to say; it’s about developing the leader you already are:

Presence, not perfect answers

It isn’t about getting every question right, but about conveying the executive presence the role demands: judgment, calm and clarity when the conversation gets difficult.

Respond, don’t react

We work on how you manage your emotions and decisions under pressure, so you respond creatively instead of reacting on autopilot, using the Leadership Circle Profile® as a reference.

Know yourself so you can show yourself

Personality assessments help you understand your style, recognise your development areas honestly and arrive with a plan to work on them. That is what sets a mature leader apart.

Throughout the process we develop, among others, these capabilities:

  • Building bridges between opposing positions
  • Influence and persuasion
  • Strategic thinking, and making it visible
  • A coherent, well-built personal brand
  • Emotional intelligence under pressure
How we work

A six-step process

  1. Diagnosis

    We understand the role, your organisation’s competency framework and every stage of the process. And your starting point: your style, your strengths and what holds you back today.

  2. Your story and your evidence

    We select and work on your real achievements for each competency. This is the foundation for the competency-based interview and the written self-reflection exercises.

  3. Practice under pressure

    Role plays, uncomfortable questions, difficult situations and cases, the business case and the recorded video interview. We practise until it stops feeling like a threat.

  4. Emotions and decisions

    After each simulation we look at what happened inside, not just what you said: how you decide, when you react, and how to find a more creative response.

  5. Talking about yourself honestly

    We work on your personality profile so you can discuss your results naturally in the reflection session, and present your development areas together with a plan.

  6. Ready for the day

    How to manage your energy through a demanding day, recover between exercises and reach the final panel interview with the same clarity you started with.

What to expect

What an executive assessment center usually includes

Each organisation designs its own, often with an external consultancy, but these are the most common exercises and what they usually observe. To go deeper, read the full preparation guide.

Competency-based interview

Real examples from your career, assessed against the role’s competency framework.

Role play

A simulated difficult conversation (with a team member, a client or a peer) to observe how you influence, listen and handle tension.

Business case

Analysing a situation, making decisions and presenting them with strategic judgment, often under time pressure.

Psychometric tests

Personality and reasoning questionnaires. There are no right answers: consistency is what counts.

Written self-reflection

Questions about your achievements, lessons and leadership style, answered in writing before or during the process.

Recorded video interview

Video questions with no one on the other side. It demands presence on camera.

Reflection session

A closing conversation about your results, where self-awareness and openness to feedback are assessed.

Panel interview

In many processes, the final stage after passing the assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Before we start

Will you teach me how to pass the psychometric tests?

No. Personality tests have no right answers, and trying to guess what’s expected usually reduces consistency. What we do is make sure you know your profile well, are familiar with the format and can talk about your results honestly.

How far in advance should I start?

Ideally around 6 months, especially if you’re preparing for your next step without a fixed date. If the process has already been announced, I work with a minimum of 8 weeks in an intensive format.

Do you work with my organisation’s competency framework?

Yes. The starting point is the specific role, its competency framework and the stages of the process. Everything we practise is aligned with what your organisation will assess.

How is this different from interview preparation?

Interview preparation helps you answer better. This process helps you lead better: executive presence, emotional management, influence and strategic thinking. We work with the assessment as the horizon, but it stays with you in the new role.

Does my company need to know I’m working with a coach?

No. I work individually with each executive, under the International Coaching Federation (ICF) Code of Ethics, and what we discuss is confidential.

Are the sessions online?

Mostly online, with executives of multinational companies in different countries. In person in Barcelona too. In English, Spanish or Catalan.

Do you have a promotion process ahead?

Tell me where you are, which role and what timeline. If we’re a fit, I’ll propose how to work on it.
